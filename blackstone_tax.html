Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Honest answers to the questions we hear most often. If you don't see yours here, call us.

Can you guarantee you will settle my debt? +
No — and any firm that makes that promise is not being honest with you. The IRS makes the final decision on every resolution, not us. What we can guarantee is a thorough investigation of your account, identification of every program you qualify for, and professional representation by licensed enrolled agents. We only recommend what has a realistic chance of approval.
How much will you save me? +
We cannot tell you a specific number until we pull your official IRS transcripts and complete a full financial analysis. Any company that quotes you a savings amount before seeing your IRS account is guessing. After our investigation we will know exactly what the IRS is likely to accept — and that is when you get a real answer, not a marketing number.
How does the Offer in Compromise work? +
An Offer in Compromise allows qualifying taxpayers to settle their IRS debt for less than the full amount owed. The IRS evaluates your income, expenses, and assets using a formula called Reasonable Collection Potential. If your RCP is less than your total debt, you may qualify for an OIC at or near that amount. The IRS rejects approximately 60% of OIC applications, which is why proper preparation and documentation matter significantly.
What if I haven't filed my tax returns? +
Unfiled returns must be addressed before any IRS resolution program can begin. This is not unusual — many of our clients have multiple years of unfiled returns when they first contact us. Our licensed professionals prepare and file all delinquent returns accurately as part of your overall resolution strategy. We handle this as part of the engagement so you can move forward.
How long does the process take? +
The investigation phase typically takes 5 to 10 business days after we receive your signed authorization and payment. Penalty abatement and installment agreements often resolve within 30 to 90 days. Offer in Compromise cases are the most complex and typically take 12 to 24 months for the IRS to fully process. We keep you updated throughout the entire timeline.
What does the consultation cost? +
The initial consultation is completely free. There is no obligation and no pressure. During the consultation we ask about your situation, explain your potential options in plain language, and let you decide whether you want to move forward with the investigation phase. You are never locked in to anything at the consultation stage.
Can the IRS take my house or my paycheck? +
Yes — the IRS has broad collection authority including wage garnishment, bank levies, and property liens. However, once licensed representation is established, many of these actions can be paused while your case is being worked. If you have an active wage garnishment or a bank levy, contact us immediately — these situations require urgent attention and can often be halted quickly once proper paperwork is filed.
What is the difference between the investigation fee and the resolution fee? +
The investigation fee covers pulling your IRS transcripts and completing a full financial analysis — including calculating your Reasonable Collection Potential and identifying every program you qualify for. Once the investigation is complete we present you with the results and a clear resolution plan. Only at that point do you decide whether to proceed with the resolution phase, which has its own fee based on your program type and complexity.
